# CRO by business model - what is right on one page and wrong on another
Written 25 September 2026 from research/research-cro-by-business-model.md (52 sources) · read by the business-fit items of the audit
Next: set the four business lines in context/business.md; the audit reads this file against them.

The same layer can be proof on one page and a red flag on the next. A portfolio grid sells a landscaper and confuses a SaaS buyer. A calendar in a lawyer's hero loses the qualification step. So the audit asks the model first, then judges every layer against these lists. Five models; when a page does not fit any, pick the nearest and say so.

## Local trade or home service (plumber, roofer, HVAC, electrician, cleaner)

- Needs:
  - business name and a tel: link in the hero
  - licence and insurance with the number
  - rating with count linked to Google
  - three ticks (years, jobs, guarantee)
  - a four-field form with the phone-reason line
  - something true about price
  - homeowner testimonials
  - the service area
  - an FAQ
  - the mobile call bar
  - a thank-you page with the callback time
- Optional: before-and-after gallery (renovation, landscaping, painting, roofing, flooring), manufacturer marks, a financing line, a founder video, a text button for an under-45 audience
- Wrong:
  - commercial client logos
  - stock lifestyle photos
  - a numbers-only case-study grid
  - a calendar on the page
  - a chat widget
  - security badges
  - countdown timers
  - quote-only pricing with no number
- Proof that wins: the Google rating with its count beside the licence number, then homeowner reviews with a first name and a suburb
- Price: on the page - a call-out fee, a typical range, or three priced jobs (78% more likely to call when pricing is online)
- Mobile bar: Call on the left, Get a quote on the right
- Form: 4 fields plus one dispatch chip where it helps
- Benchmarks (LocaliQ 2026, paid search, conversion undefined):
  - plumbing 7.63% · HVAC 6.56 to 7.48% · electricians 9.08%
  - roofing 3.70% · cleaning 17.65%
  - Unbounce home improvement: 2.6% median, 19.5% top quartile
- Word ceiling: 600

## Local professional service (lawyer, dentist, clinic, accountant)

- Needs:
  - a tel: link (legal) or a booking path (dental, clinic)
  - one credential line
  - rating with count linked to Google
  - the free-consultation or new-patient offer stated
  - a four-field form with a service chip
  - a price statement (flat fee, "from", contingency wording, or free consult plus the process)
  - written testimonials with the platform named
  - an FAQ on fees
  - process and timeline
  - the mobile bar
  - a thank-you page with the calendar
- Optional:
  - face-to-camera video
  - case results where the bar allows
  - insurance-accepted logos (dental, clinic)
  - before-and-after (cosmetic only)
  - a booking widget on the page for routine dental
  - a lead magnet for injury and family law
- Wrong:
  - a portfolio grid
  - a three-tier price table for litigation
  - a cold calendar in a lawyer's hero
  - phone-only contact for a dentist
  - stock gavels and stock smiles
  - Yelp badges
  - commercial logos
  - security badges
  - chat widgets
  - countdown timers
- Proof that wins: the Google rating with count over the ten-review floor, then the credential line
- Price: on the page as above; quote-only only for litigation and complex tax, with the free consult named
- Mobile bar: legal - Call left, Free case review right; dental - Call left, Book online right; accountant - Call left, Get a quote right
- Form: 4 fields plus one practice-area chip
- Benchmarks: legal 5.55% (LocaliQ 2026), dentists 10.67%, general dentistry 7.74%, orthodontics 14.21%; 56.3% of legal conversions are calls (Ruler)
- Word ceiling: 800

## Agency or B2B service (marketing agency, consultancy, dev shop)

- Needs:
  - an outcome headline with a number
  - three named case studies with a number and a timeframe
  - a third-party review profile with score and count (Clutch, G2, Google)
  - a qualification form of four to six fields with chips
  - the calendar right after a qualified submission
  - a price statement ("from", minimum engagement or range)
  - an FAQ on price
  - contract
  - who does the work and ownership
  - a desktop layout given equal care
  - a thank-you page
- Optional:
  - a logo strip of real recognisable clients
  - partner badges below the fold
  - a founder video
  - a client-on-camera case study
  - an audit or calculator lead magnet
  - SOC 2 near the form for dev shops with enterprise buyers
  - the phone number in the legal line
- Wrong:
  - the phone as the primary CTA
  - licence and insurance in the hero
  - Yelp or BBB badges
  - before-and-after galleries
  - a portfolio grid on a PPC or SEO agency
  - press logos in place of outcomes
  - a cold calendar with no qualification
  - a textarea
  - countdown timers
  - "free trial" language
- Proof that wins: the case study with a number and a timeframe, beside a linked third-party score with count
- Price: "from $X a month", a minimum engagement, or a three-tier typical range
- Mobile bar: Book a call (form) left, See results (case-study anchor) right
- Form: 4 to 6 fields, budget and company size as chips; multi-step allowed
- Benchmarks:
  - business services 4.85% (LocaliQ 2026); professional services 6.1% (Unbounce, Ruler); 95% of agency conversions are forms; form-to-meeting 30%
  - 66.7% with instant booking (Chili Piper)
- Word ceiling: 900

## E-commerce or physical product

- Needs:
  - product images in scale and on a model
  - the price with shipping and tax estimated on the page
  - add-to-cart above the fold
  - a sticky add-to-cart on mobile
  - on-page reviews with count
  - distribution and customer photos
  - the returns policy visible
  - the shipping time stated
  - a third-party rating
  - an FAQ on shipping
  - returns and sizing
  - express pay and guest checkout
- Optional: product-in-use video (click to play), a two-line founder note below the buy box, customer video, certifications beside the product, a Q&A block, a trust seal at the payment step
- Wrong:
  - a portfolio or case-study grid
  - a calendar
  - a consult form
  - a founder video above the product
  - licence or insurance lines
  - a commercial logo wall
  - "call now"
  - stock lifestyle shots in place of product photos
  - trust seals in the hero
  - price or shipping hidden until checkout
- Proof that wins: on-page reviews with photos and count, plus a third-party rating
- Price: stated, always, with the total cost on the product page
- Mobile bar: Add to cart with price and variant, one button; a second only for express pay
- Form: none before purchase; guest checkout, no account wall
- Benchmarks: Unbounce e-commerce 4.2% for the page action; Shopify purchase 1.4% average, 3.2% top 20%; add-to-cart 4.6% median; cart abandonment 70% (Baymard)
- Word ceiling: 700

## SaaS, app, course or community

- Needs:
  - an outcome headline
  - a trial or demo CTA above the fold
  - the price or pricing model on the page (course and community: price and payment plan)
  - an interactive demo or 60-second product video within one scroll
  - a third-party rating with count (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot)
  - three dated customer or student results with numbers
  - an FAQ on pricing
  - cancellation
  - security and onboarding (course: refund, time, access, who it is not for)
  - a thank-you page with the calendar for demo requests
- Optional:
  - a real client logo strip
  - security badges near the form
  - a founder video
  - a webinar for courses over about $500
  - a card-required trial as a quality lever
  - a phone number in the FAQ for enterprise buyers
  - a community member count
- Wrong:
  - a licence or insurance line
  - a "call now" hero
  - Yelp or BBB badges
  - before-and-after galleries
  - a cold calendar in the hero
  - quote-only pricing for self-serve
  - a long sales video for a cheap tool
  - a portfolio grid (except design tools)
  - stock office photos
  - countdown timers on evergreen offers
  - a service-area map
  - a same-day promise
- Proof that wins: the product itself (ungated demo or trial) plus a third-party rating with count; for courses, dated student results with numbers
- Price: "from $X per seat per month" for self-serve; the pricing model named for sales-led; price and payment plan always for courses
- Mobile bar: Start free trial (or Book a demo) left, See it work right; course - Enrol left, Watch the preview right
- Form: trial 1 to 2 fields; demo 4 to 6 with company size as a chip; course - checkout only
- Benchmarks: SaaS 3.8% (Unbounce), online courses 18.3%, education 13.14% (LocaliQ 2026), software 7.6% (Ruler); free-to-paid 8% median
- Word ceiling: 900

## Rules that hold for every model

- A rating average without its count fails everywhere.
- A chat widget is flagged everywhere: our own split test read 11.3% with one against 19.7% without, and the one controlled study read minus 8.6% signups.
- The benchmark quoted is the model's line above, never the all-industry 6.6% or 8%, and the source's conversion definition is printed beside it.
- When the model is unknown, the audit infers it from the conversion action, prints the guess, and asks.
  - tel: in the hero means local · add-to-cart means e-commerce
  - trial or demo means SaaS · form plus calendar means agency
- Numbers that did not survive the research are never printed: "VSLs convert 2 to 3x", "sticky cart +18 to 32%", "badges +10 to 20%", "chatbots 3x", "43% of homeowners choose on price".
