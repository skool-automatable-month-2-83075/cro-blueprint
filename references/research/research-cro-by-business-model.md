Scope: what changes on the page when the business model changes. Nothing settled in research-landing-page-2026.md (form spec, autofill, consent, speed, chat-widget script weight, NN/g price findings, LocaliQ home-services table) is repeated except where a model overrides it or the number is needed beside another model's number. Five models: (1) local trade or home service, (2) local professional service, (3) agency or B2B service, (4) e-commerce or physical product, (5) SaaS, app, course or community.

Grades: (a) official documentation, platform data, or a study with 10,000+ pages, sessions, campaigns or submissions · (b) named study or survey with a stated sample, or a documented A/B test · (c) practitioner claim, roundup, vendor page without method, or a number whose primary could not be reached. Vendor-owned datasets carry (a, vendor): the sample is real, the population is the vendor's own customers.

## Findings

### Model 1 - local trade or home service (plumber, roofer, HVAC, electrician, cleaner)

**1. What the buyer judges on**
- Roofing Contractor 2025 (national homeowner survey with ROOFLE and Owens Corning, sample not stated, mean age 56): experience and reputation is the most important factor for 34%, professional licensing is named by 41%, online reviews are "very" or "extremely" important to 67%; 79% find roofers through word of mouth, 62% through internet search, 34% through Home Advisor or Angi (b)
- Same survey: preferred contact is phone 60%, email 26%, virtual meeting 9%, text 4%; about 40% call poor communication the biggest problem with contractors; 67% say communication decides between firms (b)
- Roofing Contractor 2024 (snippet only): insurance coverage, long warranties and low bids each under 10% as a top factor. Insurance is table stakes, one line, not a section
- Modernize 2018 (2,500 homeowners, lead-gen vendor): 43% choose on price, 28% on reviews. Old, vendor-run, and the 2025 roofing survey ranks price under reputation and licensing (b, weak)
- Houzz snippets: 62% name recommendations, references and reviews together; 48% struggle to find someone they trust; 34% want clear proposals. Houzz Pro's own checklist puts reviews, photos of completed projects and licensing in the top four with no survey behind it (c)
- BrightLocal 2025 (1,026 US adults): Google is the review platform for 85%, Facebook 54%, Yelp 39%, BBB 32%; 42% trust reviews as much as personal recommendations, down from 79% in 2020; 46% would be suspicious of an AI-written review; responding to all reviews, not a selection, raises likelihood of use (b). The rating line links to Google
- Portfolio: no controlled test of a before-and-after gallery on a trade page was found in this pass. "Judged by looking" stays a house ruling for visual trades (renovation, landscaping, painting, roofing, flooring) and does not extend to emergency repair, where the buyer is judging speed and legitimacy (c)
- The proof format with evidence behind it is the rating with its count. Baymard's five surveys (5,170 respondents) had nearly twice as many people prefer a 4.5 average from 57 ratings over a 5.0 from 4; one participant: "I'd rather have something that has 700 reviews and four stars because then I know people still buy it" (b, e-commerce display, same mechanism)

**2. Conversion action and page furniture**
- Call plus a short form. Ruler 2026 (110M sessions) has no home-services line, but its two nearest categories run calls at 56.3% (legal) and 52.6% (professional services), with the note that "higher-stakes decisions or more sensitive conversations tend to drive a higher proportion of calls" (a); the roofing buyer prefers phone 60% (b). LocaliQ's lead definition is "phone, chat, form fill, or email", so its home-services conversion rates already contain calls (a)
- The phone number matters most in this model of the five: hero tel: link, sticky bar, and the ad's call asset. Hiding it behind a form is the wrong layer
- Calendar: not on the page. No new evidence this pass; the thank-you page ruling stands (c). An emergency buyer wants a human now, not a slot
- Mobile bar: call left, form right; text replaces form only where the owner answers texts and the audience skews under 45 (prior dossier age split: 55+ call seven to one, 25 to 34 text almost as often as they call)

**3. Where price belongs**
- Roofing Contractor 2025: 78% of homeowners are more likely to call when pricing appears online, and only 25% of contractors put pricing online (b). The strongest single price finding in this pass for any model
- Format: call-out fee, typical range, or three priced example jobs. Quote-only with no number is the exit (NN/g, prior dossier, b). Ruling unchanged: a true statement about money, not necessarily a price
- Roofing (3.70% CVR) and doors and windows (4.41%) are the lowest-converting trades in LocaliQ's table (a); they are quote-shopping buyers, and a range on the page is what stops the third tab

**4. Licence, insurance, certifications, badges**
- Licence number and "insured" in the hero: licensing 41% (b); hiding qualifications is an unacceptable business practice under Google Ads policy (prior, a). Bond only where the state requires it
- Badges: Google rating with count; BBB if accredited (32% still use it, b). Manufacturer certifications (GAF Master Elite, Carrier or Lennox dealer, Generac) are the one logo type that fits a trade page because they imply warranty; no test measures them (c)
- Not above the fold: commercial client logos, "as seen on" press logos, generic satisfaction seals

**5. Form, lead magnet, chat**
- Four fields (house). One qualifying chip only where dispatch needs it (repair or install; leak or remodel). LocaliQ home-services CPL median $90.92 and plumbing $129.02 put a dollar figure on every abandoned form (a)
- Lead magnet: weak for emergency work (the visitor is mid-leak), fits considered purchases: a roof replacement price guide, a window quote checklist, a "what a furnace should cost in [city]" one-pager (c, follows from the 3.70% and 4.41% CVRs)
- Chat: Jono's house split test on a service page, 11.3% with the widget against 19.7% without (b, single). Kissmetrics 2014: Olark on features, customers and pricing pages cut signups 8.59% at 81% significance and activations 22.14% at 97% (b, single, SaaS). Databox's "forms were not cannibalised" is one respondent with no numbers (c). No widget on a trade ad page

**6. Page length, video, FAQ**
- Unbounce commercial and professional services 2024 (part of the 41,000-page set): median 6.1%; 275 to 745 words; grade 7 or below; desktop 11.6% against mobile 8.3% with mobile 81% of visits; "repair and maintenance services pages convert almost 3x better than home and renovation services" (a). Repair buyers are mid-problem, renovation buyers are comparing. Page length follows: short for repair, longer with a portfolio and a range for renovation
- Founder video: still no controlled test (prior). Click-to-play, poster as LCP. Video testimonials of homeowners at their own front door beat a studio
- FAQ: price, arrival time, licence and insurance, guarantee, what happens after the call, service area, payment options. Not "our story"

**7. Benchmarks 2025 to 2026**
- LocaliQ home services, Apr 2024 to Mar 2025, 3,211 campaigns, min 103 per category, conversion undefined (a): plumbing CVR 7.63% and CPL $129.02; roofing and gutters 3.70% and $228.15; AC 6.56% and $127.74; electricians 9.08% and $93.69; cleaning 17.65% and $46.99; handyman 13.45% and $54.05; landscaping 6.42% and $117.92; painting 10.80% and $138.38; garage doors 5.66% and $81.45; doors and windows 4.41% and $200.34; general contractors 2.61% and $165.67
- LocaliQ 2026 all-industry edition (thousands of campaigns): Home and Home Improvement CTR 6.47%, CPC $8.33, CVR 8.05%, CPL $90.92; overall CPL fell "for the first time in five years" (a)
- Unbounce 2024: home improvement 2.6% median, 19.5% top quartile (prior dossier); commercial and professional services 6.1% (a)
- First Page Sage (80+ SEO clients, MQL definition): HVAC 3.1%, construction 1.9% (b). Organic traffic and a lead-quality filter, not comparable to LocaliQ
- Ruler 2026: no home-services line; overall 5.13% (a)
- Definition caveat: LocaliQ counts any contact from a click; Unbounce counts the page's action; FPS counts an MQL. Compare a page to LocaliQ's trade line for paid search, never to the all-industry 8.18%

**8. What is wrong for this model**
- Commercial logo wall on a residential page (no controlled evidence for logo walls, prior; the buyer is a homeowner and recognises no brands)
- Stock lifestyle photography: 46% are already suspicious of AI-written reviews (b) and the homeowner is checking that the van, the crew and the jobs are real. Real jobs or a designed substitute, never stock
- A numbers-driven case-study grid ("+212% ROI in 90 days") - a B2B format the homeowner cannot read
- Calendar in the hero; chat widget; "contact us for a quote" as the only price statement; text-only contact for a 55+ buyer; booking-first for emergency trades
- SOC 2 or security badges; "trusted by 500 companies"; countdown timers

### Model 2 - local professional service (lawyer, dentist, accountant, clinic)

**1. What the buyer judges on**
- FindLaw 2024 US Consumer Legal Needs Survey (2,000 adults with a legal need): 97% of those who researched online used a search engine; 82% relied on online reviews; 56% act within a week (b). Search summary of the same survey: 40% named reviews their primary source and 50% said reviews played a significant role; expertise, specialisation, cost and reputation are the top factors, expertise first (snippet only)
- Martindale-Avvo (2018 Avvo study, sample not on page): 48% say a free consultation is important; price is "the most valuable decision criteria"; consumers "want to benchmark costs when researching online" and want to know "what's included in a price and whether or not rates are negotiable" (b, old)
- Clio 2024 secret shopper (500 US firms): 40% answered the phone (56% in 2019), 33% replied to email (40% in 2019), 48% were unreachable by phone; prospects who reached someone by phone were over three times as likely to recommend the firm; 7% of firms use a chatbot; 51% of prospects see a chatbot as a helpful start, three in five only if they can switch to a human (b)
- Dentaly Oct 2023 (1,099 Americans, self-report): 83% use reviews to evaluate a dentist and 8 in 10 as the first step; Google 85%, Yelp 37%, Healthgrades 25%; 52% will only consider a dentist with at least 10 reviews; quality of treatment 90%, cost 59%, location 46%, range of services 40%, customer support 38%, waiting times 27%, review responses 22%; 3 in 10 would go out of network for a highly reviewed clinic (b)
- Tebra 2025 (3,964 US adults, fielded 29 Jun 2025): 79% read reviews before choosing a provider; 53% will not consider one under 4 stars; 44% say the practice website influences the decision; 56% search Google, 41% practice websites, 38% WebMD; 82% give a provider one or two chances before switching; 29% cite unresponsive staff (b)
- Proof form that wins: the Google rating with count (the 10-review floor is explicit for dentists), then a credential line (expertise is the top legal factor). Case results with numbers are the lawyer's case study where the bar allows; before-and-after is the cosmetic dentist's portfolio and nobody else's in this model (c)

**2. Conversion action and page furniture**
- Ruler 2026 (a): legal 56.3% calls and 43.7% forms; health and social care 62.8% forms and 37.2% calls; professional services 52.6% calls. Lawyer: call first, form second. Dentist and clinic: form or booking first, call second. Accountant: form and call level
- Tebra: 43% prefer online booking and 41% prefer the phone for scheduling; 34% say online scheduling is important; 65% would switch providers for better digital features (b). Oral Health Group roundup: about a third of online dental bookings happen when the office is closed (ADA, c, 2021). A booking widget is the dentist's conversion action for routine and hygiene visits; for implants, Invisalign and anything over a few hundred dollars the consult form comes first and the calendar sits on the thank-you page
- Legal: form, then calendar, never cold. The firm checks conflicts and case type before it gives a slot. Clio's 40% answer rate says the real leak is picking up, not booking (b)
- Mobile bar: legal call left, form right; dental call left, "Book online" right (widget or form); accountant call left, form right

**3. Where price belongs**
- Clio 2024 secret shopper: on the phone only 41% offered rate information, 12% could estimate total cost, 36% could explain the process and next steps (b). Fees and process are the two things prospects say they cannot find on firm websites (Clio guide, snippet only)
- Clio 2025 solo and small firm report (snippet only): 75% of solo and 65% of small firms now offer flat fees. Where a flat fee exists it goes on the page; contingency work gets "no fee unless we win" in the ad's exact wording; hourly work gets "consultations from $X" or a typical matter range
- Dental: cost is a factor for 59% (b). "New patient exam and x-rays $X" and "implants from $X" are the price statements; the insurance-accepted list is the second one
- Quote-only is acceptable for litigation and complex tax with a stated free consultation. It is not acceptable for routine dental, flat-fee family law or bookkeeping

**4. Licence, insurance, certifications, badges**
- One credential line above the fold: bar admission or specialisation, years, board certification, a named award with its year. Expertise is the top legal factor (snippet) and quality of treatment is 90% for dental (b). A line, not a wall
- Insurance-accepted logos above the fold for dental and clinics is the one logo wall that is right in this model, because the buyer filters on it before anything else (cost 59%, out-of-network stops 7 in 10, b, application c)
- Licence numbers are not needed; the profession implies them. A malpractice-insurance line reads as a warning (c)
- Volume claims ("$500M recovered", "2,000 smiles") are this model's case-study number. Bar advertising rules restrict superlatives in many states, so the audit checks the wording against the rules, not against CRO

**5. Form, lead magnet, chat**
- Four fields plus one chip: practice area or service. That chip routes intake and reads as the visitor's benefit (the prior dossier's exception). Never a case-description textarea on the ad page; that belongs on the call
- Lead magnet: "the first 48 hours after a crash" fits PI; a fee guide fits family law; a new-patient offer is the dental magnet. Bankruptcy and tax already convert over 13% (a) and do not need one
- Chat: 7% of firms use chatbots and 3 in 5 prospects only want one with a human handoff (b). Jono's 11.3% against 19.7% on a service page (b, single). No widget; if the firm runs 24/7 intake, the phone line and form already are the channel

**6. Page length, video, FAQ**
- Unbounce legal 2024: median 6.3%; disability 6.3%, family 6.3%, immigration 5.6%; mobile is 88% of traffic and converts 32% better than desktop (21% against 15.9%); about 600 words; the one industry where college-level copy converts best, 7.2% (a). Do not carry that reading-level finding into the other four models
- Founder video: attorney or dentist face to camera fits the expertise factor; still no controlled test (c). Keep it click-to-play under the hero
- FAQ: fees and how billing works, timeline, what happens on the first call, insurance accepted, "do I have a case", hours and parking for clinics

**7. Benchmarks 2025 to 2026**
- LocaliQ legal (Apr 2022 to Mar 2023, 256 campaigns, min 10 per practice area, b): overall CTR 4.76%, CPC $9.21, CVR 7%, CPL $111.05; PI 5.45% and $159.17; criminal 9.90% and $101.49; bankruptcy 13.56% and $82.27; estate and probate 9.65% and $72.24; family 8.52% and $103.54; tax 13.30% and $120.30; general practice 5.52% and $96.54
- LocaliQ 2026 all-industry (a): attorneys and legal CTR 5.87%, CPC $9.87, CVR 5.55%, CPL $131.63, the highest CPL of any industry; dentists CTR 5.66%, CPC $8.00, CVR 10.67%, CPL $72.97; health and fitness CVR 6.94%, CPL $67.36
- LocaliQ healthcare (Oct 2024 to Sep 2025, 3,542 campaigns, min 43 per specialty, a): general dentistry CVR 7.74% and CPL $84.77; emergency dentistry 8.89% and $75.19; orthodontics 14.21% and $71.52; physical therapy 15.35% and $32.79; dermatology 25.33% and $18.54
- Unbounce 2024 (a): legal 6.3%; commercial and professional services 6.1%
- Ruler 2026 (a): legal 7.9%, professional services 6.1%, health and social care 2.3%, defined as a qualified lead or sale across forms, calls and chat
- First Page Sage (SEO, MQL, b): legal 3.4%, the highest in their set
- Definition caveat: LocaliQ legal is a 2022 to 2023 window on 256 campaigns and should be quoted with its date; the 2026 5.55% is the current line

**8. What is wrong for this model**
- A portfolio grid on a law page (nothing to look at); before-and-after on a general dentist's page (right for cosmetic only)
- A three-tier price table for litigation; conversely, "contact us for pricing" for routine dental or bookkeeping
- A cold calendar in a lawyer's hero (conflict check first); phone-only for a dentist when 43% prefer to book online (b)
- Stock gavel, scales, handshake, stock smile; a Yelp badge for a lawyer when Google is 85% (b)
- SOC 2 badges; commercial client logos; "trusted by 500 companies"; countdown timers on anything medical or legal

### Model 3 - agency or B2B service (marketing agency, consultancy, dev shop)

**1. What the buyer judges on**
- TrustRadius 2026 B2B Buying Disconnect (1,862 buyers, 444 vendors): 74% consulted customer reviews; 63% used review sites (58% in 2025); 53% consulted peers and 100% of them found it helpful; product demos were the deciding factor for 18% (13% before); vendor marketing collateral ranked last of the resources consulted; 47% trust online resources less than a year ago; buyers favoured "the safest, most trusted option" (b)
- Clutch B2B buyer data (snippet only, page 404): 25% rank transparent cost information the most important element of a services company's website, case studies and testimonials 23%; 94% use reviews, about half read six; evaluation runs two to three months
- Demand Gen Report Content Preferences (snippet only, 403): case studies 78%, webinars 58%, user reviews 57%, analyst reports 54%; 61% consume three to seven pieces before talking to sales
- Setup 2024 Marketing Relationship Survey (sample not stated): referrals are the most common discovery route; 75% find agency selection time-consuming; 77% say it is not simple; 48% were unhappy with their agency's delivery (c)
- Proof form that wins: the case study with a number, a timeframe and a named client, beside a linked third-party profile (Clutch, G2, Google) with count. A portfolio only for shops judged by looking: design, branding, video, web design. A dev shop shows shipped products with a line of outcome; a consultancy shows outcomes only (c)

**2. Conversion action and page furniture**
- Ruler 2026: marketing and advertising 95.1% forms, 4.9% calls (a). The phone number is nearly irrelevant here; it can live in the legal line. A tel: link in the hero is not harmful, it is just not the action
- Chili Piper 2025 (nearly 4M submissions, vendor customers, a, vendor): 30% of form submissions book a meeting without instant scheduling against 66.7% of qualified submissions when a scheduler appears right after the form; a live-call option 69.2%; 14.1% of submissions disqualified as spam, personal emails or unqualified. Form first, calendar immediately after, only for qualified submissions. A cold calendar in the hero skips the filter that removes 14% of submissions before a human sees them
- Mobile bar: "Book a call" (form anchor) left, "See results" (case-study anchor) right (c). Text or WhatsApp only if the shop actually answers them

**3. Where price belongs**
- Transparent cost is the top website element for 25% of B2B buyers (Clutch, snippet only); NN/g B2B: price is the number one information need and users left for competitors when it was missing (prior, b). "From $X/month", a minimum engagement, or a typical range for three project types
- Retainers and productised services get the number; custom builds get "projects from $X, typical range $X to $Y"; quote-only survives only with the range stated (c)

**4. Licence, insurance, certifications, badges**
- Third-party review badges with score and count (Clutch, G2) are the credential; vendor-controlled assets ranked last with buyers (b). Platform partner badges (Google Partner, Meta Business Partner, HubSpot, Shopify Plus) sit below the fold; no test shows they lift (c)
- Licence and insurance lines: not above the fold. Professional indemnity matters only to enterprise procurement and lives in the FAQ (c)
- SOC 2 belongs to the dev shop with enterprise buyers, near the form, not in the hero (c)

**5. Form, lead magnet, chat**
- The one model where qualification fields earn their place: First Page Sage, "for demo requests, add additional fields like job title or phone number" (b); Apexure runs name, email, company, role, company size behind "Book my demo" and reports multi-step forms completing 20 to 30% more often (c); Chili Piper's 14.1% disqualification is why budget or company size as chips is worth its cost when the calendar follows. Four to six fields, chips not dropdowns, budget as a chip, never a textarea
- Lead magnet: the gated PDF is collapsing (Shno roundup citing HubSpot 2024: gated ebooks below 0.9%, interactive tools 5.2%+, quizzes 20 to 40%, c); TrustRadius: buyers want to self-serve (b). The magnet is an audit, teardown, calculator or benchmark, delivered on the thank-you page
- Chat: Kissmetrics 2014 on pricing and features pages, signups down 8.59% and activations down 22.14% with Olark (b, single); Jono's 11.3% against 19.7% (b, single); Databox anecdote that Drift did not cannibalise forms, no numbers (c). No widget by default; a staffed chat on a pricing page is a test, not a rule

**6. Page length, video, FAQ**
- Unbounce commercial and professional services 2024: 275 to 745 words, grade 7, desktop 11.6% against mobile 8.3% (a). B2B buyers convert at work on desktop; this is the only model where the desktop layout gets equal care to mobile
- Video: the client on camera saying the number is the case study; a founder video answers "who will I actually work with" (c)
- FAQ: price and contract length, who does the work (senior or junior), reporting cadence, ownership of ad accounts and code, what happens after the call

**7. Benchmarks 2025 to 2026**
- LocaliQ 2026 (a): business services CTR 6.10%, CPC $5.87, CVR 4.85%, CPL $93.69; industrial and commercial CVR 8.20%, CPL $75.19
- Unbounce 2024 (a): commercial and professional services 6.1%; no agency cut exists (the agency URL returns 404)
- Ruler 2026 (a): professional services 6.1%, software 7.6%, overall 5.13%
- First Page Sage (b): business consulting 1.7%, IT and managed services 1.5%, software development 1.1% (SEO, MQL); B2B page types (83 companies, 2019 to 2026): customer-type 3.5%, application 3.1%, product 2.9%, service 2.7%, industry 1.8%, location 1.1%
- Chili Piper (a, vendor): form-to-meeting 30% baseline, 66.7% with instant booking
- Definition caveat: a booked meeting, an MQL and a form fill are three different numerators; the page benchmark is LocaliQ business services 4.85% for paid search

**8. What is wrong for this model**
- Phone as the primary CTA (4.9% of conversions); licence and insurance in the hero; Yelp or BBB badges
- A before-and-after gallery on a consultancy page; a portfolio grid for a PPC agency (show the numbers)
- "As seen on" press logos in place of client outcomes; countdown timers; "free trial" language for a service
- A calendar in the hero with no qualification; a 12-field form; a textarea; a "how did you hear about us" dropdown

### Model 4 - e-commerce or physical product

**1. What the buyer judges on**
- Baymard Product Page UX 2026 (30,000+ rated pages across 155+ US and EU sites, a): 42% of users try to judge product size from the images; 37% of sites have no in-scale image; 23% no human model for worn products; 67% do not show total order cost on the PDP; 81% no unit price; 44% do not show or link the returns policy; 15% abandoned an order over an unsatisfactory returns policy; 89% do not respond to negative reviews; 63% block navigation across customer photos; "users rely on customer images to validate that site photos are truly representative"; only 48% of desktop and 38% of mobile PDPs rate decent or better
- Baymard article collection (a): after product photos, reviews are the most used part of the page; 65% of sites get the ratings distribution summary wrong; 43% do not show estimated shipping; 35% embed product video badly; 83% of apparel sites give insufficient sizing
- Baymard ratings count (5,170+ respondents, b): a 4.5 from 57 ratings beats a 5.0 from 4 nearly two to one; only 5% of sites now omit the count next to the average (25% in 2015)
- Clutch Feb 2026 (400 US consumers, b): 96% check reviews before a first-time purchase; 47% always do for an unfamiliar brand; 72% will not consider under four stars; only 15% trust star ratings alone; 43% want stars with written feedback and 34% photos; reviews on Amazon trusted by 73%, Google 65%, brand-owned sites 46%; 72% say suspected AI involvement lowers trust
- Proof form that wins: on-page reviews with customer photos, count and distribution, plus a third-party rating (Google, Trustpilot, Amazon) because the brand's own review page is trusted by under half. Case studies and portfolios: wrong

**2. Conversion action and page furniture**
- Add to cart, with total cost visible before the click (67% fail, a). The buyer of a $40 product does not call; the phone number is a footer line for returns
- Sticky add-to-cart: GrowthRock supplement store (about 2,000 conversions per arm, 99% significance) +7.9% orders and +8.6% add-to-carts on desktop; mobile drawer variant +5.2% orders at 98% and +11.8% ATC clicks, while the scroll-to-buy-box variant was not significant (b). Traction NZ +2.74% at 82.7% probability, sample not stated (c). The Good's plant-based food brand +4.09%, no sample (c). Implementation matters more than the pattern: the drawer won, the scroll-to did not
- Calendar: wrong. Consult forms: wrong except for made-to-order over about $1,000 (c)
- Dedicated ad landing page against PDP: no controlled evidence found. Thriftizer (practitioner): "a good enriched PDP beats a mediocre landing page"; the enriched PDP carries "a two-line founder note, three review screenshots with faces, a shipping and returns block written in plain language", one CTA repeated four or five times, no nav (c). The "3 to 5x" and "20 to 50%" landing-page claims are vendor snippets with no sample
- Mobile bar: the sticky add-to-cart with price and variant, one button. The Good's spec: product name, rating with count, price after variant selection, shipping time optional (c). A second button only for express pay (Shop Pay, Apple Pay) (c)

**3. Where price belongs**
- Stated, always, with shipping and tax estimated on the PDP. Extra costs are the top abandonment reason at 40% of the non-browsing segment; "couldn't see or calculate total order cost up front" 12%; slow delivery 20% (Baymard 50-study list, a). "From" only for variant ranges. Quote-only is a different business model

**4. Licence, insurance, certifications, badges**
- Baymard trust seals (b): Norton's SSL seal inspired the most confidence; general trust seals (Google Trusted Store, BBB Accredited, TRUSTe) beat most SSL seals; a homemade seal beat established vendors' SSL seals; "adding any visual icon will help increase the user's general level of perceived security". Badges work at the payment step, where 19% abandoned for not trusting the site with a card (a). In the hero they are noise
- Product certifications (organic, FDA, CE, cruelty-free) sit beside the product, not in the header (c). Licence and insurance lines: wrong

**5. Form, lead magnet, chat**
- No form before purchase. Checkout: forced account creation 18%, too long or complicated 17%, site errors 17% (a); 21% rely on save features and 89% of sites do not offer them to guests (a)
- Lead magnet: discount for email, desktop exit-intent only (prior). "Coupon pages up to 82%" is a roundup number with no primary (c)
- Chat: no PDP evidence found; a Q&A block answers the same questions without 100 to 500 KB of script (prior)

**6. Page length, video, FAQ**
- Unbounce e-commerce 2024 (a): median 4.2%; food and beverage 7.1%, auto and industrial parts 5.1%, fashion and beauty 1.3%; 285 to 930 words; grade 5 to 7 converts best at 5.6%; mobile brings 2.5x the visits and converts almost 18% less
- Video: the product in use, inside the gallery, click to play (35% embed it badly, a). UGC play-rate and "+84% with demonstration video" numbers are vendor stats (c)
- FAQ: shipping time and cost, returns, sizing, materials, warranty. 44% of sites hide the returns policy (a)

**7. Benchmarks 2025 to 2026**
- Unbounce 2024 landing pages (a): 4.2% median; the page action, not a purchase
- Littledata 2023 (2,800 Shopify stores, b): average 1.4%, top 20% over 3.2%, top 10% over 4.7%; mobile 1.2%, desktop 1.9%; fashion 1.9%, food and beverage 1.5%
- ConversionStudio roundup of Littledata (12,000+ stores) and Dynamic Yield (b, second hand): add-to-cart median 4.6%, average 7.0 to 8.5%, top 20% over 11.5%; desktop 9.8%, mobile 5.7%; ATC-to-purchase 30% (jewelry and luxury) to 52% (food and beverage)
- Ruler 2026 (a): retail and e-commerce 2.4%
- Baymard (a): 70.22% average cart abandonment across 50 studies; a 35.26% conversion gain available from checkout design alone
- First Page Sage (b): e-commerce 1.6% (SEO, lead definition, not purchase)
- Definition caveat: landing-page conversion (4.2%), store conversion (1.4%) and add-to-cart (4.6%) are three different funnels. Audit an ad PDP on add-to-cart rate against the vertical median, and the store on purchase rate

**8. What is wrong for this model**
- Portfolio or case-study grid on a category or product page; a calendar; a consult form; a founder video above the product
- Licence, insurance, "fully bonded"; commercial logo walls; "call now"
- Stock lifestyle shots in place of in-scale and on-model product photos (42% judge size from images, a)
- Trust seals in the hero instead of at payment; price hidden until cart; shipping cost hidden until checkout

### Model 5 - SaaS, app, course or community

**1. What the buyer judges on**
- TrustRadius 2026 (b): buyers rank product demos, free trials, prior experience and independent validation as preferred resources; 74% consult reviews; vendor collateral last; familiarity and market leaders won and "safest, most trusted option" rose. The proof that survives is the product itself and third-party ratings
- Navattic 2026 (40,000+ demos, about 5,000 B2B SaaS sites, a, vendor): 80% of top-performing demo CTAs are above the fold or in the navbar; 66% of top demos are ungated and ungated demos show 6% higher engagement and 7% higher completion; when gated, 75% use a single field, mostly at the end; interactive demos claim a 12% higher conversion rate than product videos; placement on product pages 62% (19% in 2024), homepages 48% (8% in 2024); multi-flow demos complete 48% more often; top 1% engagement 56%, completion 48%, CTR 71%
- Courses and communities: no controlled evidence on proof format found. Dated student results with numbers are the case study; the live member count is the community's review count (c)
- Logos: this is the model where a client logo wall fits, because the buyer recognises the brands and "safest option" won (b, application c). Real, licensed logos only

**2. Conversion action and page furniture**
- Free trial or demo, chosen by time to value: Apexure's rule, trial if the user reaches value within about 10 minutes, demo if setup or configuration is needed (c). The evidence behind the split is funnel arithmetic, not a test
- ChartMogul Feb 2026 (200 B2B products, typical respondent $1 to 10M ARR, b): per 1,000 visitors a no-card trial produces 45 signups, a card-required trial 35 signups and 10.5 paying customers; median free-to-paid 8%; no-card trial good 4 to 6% and great 10 to 15%; card-required good 25 to 35% and great 50 to 60%; freemium good 3 to 5%; reverse trial 4 to 6%
- First Page Sage SaaS funnel (50+ clients, b): visitor to lead PPC 0.7%, SEO 2.1%, LinkedIn 2.2%, webinar 0.9%; small-company targets 2.3%, enterprise 0.7%; opportunity to close 31 to 46%
- Demo path: form, then calendar immediately for qualified submissions (Chili Piper 30% to 66.7%, a, vendor). A cold calendar in the hero is wrong for the same qualification reason as agencies
- Phone number: no test found either way. Ruler's software line is not split by channel on the page; its nearest B2B category runs 95% forms (a). Ruling: no phone in the hero for self-serve; a number for enterprise or regulated buyers in the FAQ (c)
- Courses: enrol or apply; a webinar or workshop registration where the price is over about $500 (acceleroi's price bands, c). Livestorm 2025 report: 63 registrants average, 48% show up, 24 minutes engaged of 61; 17% watch live and the replay (b, sample not stated on page); ON24 60% registration to attendance (snippet only)
- Mobile bar: "Start free trial" (or "Book a demo") left, "See it work" (interactive demo or 60-second product video anchor) right (c). Course: "Enrol" left, "Watch the preview" right (c)

**3. Where price belongs**
- G2 Aug 2025 (a): only 4% of product profiles list an explicit price, still over 9,300 profiles; more than 2,300 carry non-numerical "contact us" pricing; no before-and-after conversion data. TrustRadius: up-front pricing is on the buyer's list (snippet). NN/g B2B: price the number one need (prior, b)
- Self-serve: the price or "from $X per seat per month" on the landing page. Sales-led: the pricing model named (per seat, usage, annual) even without the number. Courses and communities: the price and the payment plan, always
- No evidence that hiding price lifts anything; the G2 figure shows how common it is, not that it works

**4. Licence, insurance, certifications, badges**
- SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA near the signup or demo form, not in the hero. The "10 to 20% lift" and "12 to 34%" trust-signal numbers on SaaS Hero and similar pages trace to other blogs or to nothing (c). Baymard's "any visual icon raises perceived security" is the only test-based line, and it concerns payment forms (b)
- G2 and Capterra badges with score and count (c). Licence and insurance lines: wrong. Yelp and BBB: wrong

**5. Form, lead magnet, chat**
- Trial: email and password, or SSO. Card-required is a volume-for-quality trade (35 against 45 signups per 1,000 visitors, 10.5 paying, b). Demo: four to six fields with company size as chips; First Page Sage: early-stage forms email, first name, company optional; demo requests add job title or phone (b)
- Lead magnet: the ungated interactive demo is the magnet (Navattic, a, vendor); gated ebooks are collapsing (roundup, c); the webinar for courses (Livestorm, b)
- Chat: Kissmetrics 2014 on pricing and features pages, signups down 8.59% and activations down 22.14% (b, single); Jono's 11.3% against 19.7% (b, single). Off by default; a pricing-page test at most

**6. Page length, video, FAQ**
- Unbounce SaaS 2024 (a): 3.8% median, 42% under the 6.6% baseline; hardware 4.1%, data and infrastructure 3.3%; 250 to 725 words; 50 to 140 difficult words; grade 5 to 7 copy converts 12.9% against 2.1% for professional-level copy
- Unbounce education 2024 (a): 8.4% median; online courses 18.3%, general course pages 13%, higher education 6.3%, primary and tutoring 4.9%; 363 words, the lowest of any industry; 12th grade or lower converts 54% to over 2x better; mobile 10.2% against desktop 12% with mobile nearly 6x the traffic
- Video: interactive demo over product video (a, vendor); a founder video on a course page is the sales video, and every "VSL converts 2 to 3x" number failed verification (below)
- FAQ: pricing and cancellation, data and security, integrations, onboarding time; course: refund policy, time commitment, access length, who it is not for

**7. Benchmarks 2025 to 2026**
- Unbounce 2024 (a): SaaS 3.8%; education 8.4%; online courses 18.3%
- LocaliQ 2026 (a): education and instruction CTR 7.56%, CPC $4.81, CVR 13.14%, CPL $77.48; business services CVR 4.85%, CPL $93.69 (the closest SaaS proxy; LocaliQ has no SaaS line)
- Ruler 2026 (a): software 7.6%, education 6.3%, defined as qualified leads across forms, calls and chat
- First Page Sage (b): B2B SaaS 1.1%, software development 1.1% (SEO, MQL); PPC visitor to lead 0.7%
- ChartMogul (b): median free-to-paid 8%
- Chili Piper (a, vendor): form-to-meeting 30% baseline, 66.7% with instant booking
- Livestorm (b): 48% show-up. acceleroi (c, internal, no sources): sales page 3 to 5% under $500 and 0.5 to 2% at $500 to $1,000; opt-in 3 to 5% cold and 5 to 8% paid search; registration to attendance 30 to 40%
- Definition caveat: Unbounce counts the page action, ChartMogul counts paid within six months, FPS counts an MQL from organic, Chili Piper counts meetings from submissions. None of these is the same number, and a SaaS page is audited on signup or demo-request rate, never on the 6.6% all-industry median

**8. What is wrong for this model**
- Licence and insurance lines; a "call now" hero; Yelp or BBB badges; before-and-after galleries
- A cold calendar in the hero; quote-only pricing for a self-serve product; a 12-minute sales video for a $20 a month tool
- A portfolio grid (except template or design tools showing output); stock office photography; countdown timers on evergreen courses (misrepresentation policy, prior)

- "Gated ebooks convert below 0.9% (HubSpot 2024)", "quizzes 20 to 40%", "coupon pages up to 82%". Shno roundup; primaries not reachable. Direction (interactive beats gated PDF) kept as (c), numbers not quoted

## The model matrix

**Model 1 - local trade or home service**
- Required layers: hero with business name and tel: link, licence and insurance line with the number, rating with count linked to Google, three ticks (years, jobs, guarantee), four-field form with the phone-reason line, a true price statement, video or written testimonials from homeowners, service area, FAQ, mobile call bar, thank-you page with callback time
- Optional layers: before-and-after gallery (renovation, landscaping, painting, roofing, flooring only), manufacturer certification marks, financing line, founder video, lead magnet for roofing and windows quote-shoppers, text button on the bar for an under-45 audience
- Wrong layers: commercial client logos, stock lifestyle photos, numbers-driven case-study grid, calendar on the page, chat widget, SOC 2 or security badges, countdown timers, quote-only pricing with no number
- Proof form that wins: Google rating with count beside the licence number; homeowner testimonials with first name and suburb
- Where price goes: on the page as a call-out fee, a typical range or three priced jobs (78% more likely to call when pricing is online, b)
- Mobile bar: Call left, Get a quote (form) right; Text replaces form only by age and staffing
- Form field count: 4, plus one dispatch chip where needed
- Benchmark median: LocaliQ trade line for paid search (plumbing 7.63%, HVAC 6.56 to 7.48%, electricians 9.08%, roofing 3.70%, cleaning 17.65%); Unbounce home improvement 2.6% median and 19.5% top quartile for the page

**Model 2 - local professional service**
- Required layers: hero with tel: link (legal) or booking CTA (dental and clinic), one credential line, rating with count linked to Google, free-consultation or new-patient offer stated, four-field form with a practice-area or service chip, a price statement (flat fee, "from", contingency wording, or free consult plus process), written testimonials with platform named, FAQ covering fees, process and timeline, mobile bar, thank-you page with the calendar
- Optional layers: attorney or dentist face-to-camera video, case results with numbers where bar rules allow, insurance-accepted logos above the fold (dental and clinic), before-and-after gallery (cosmetic dentistry and aesthetics only), online booking widget on the page for routine dental and hygiene, lead magnet for PI and family law
- Wrong layers: portfolio grid on a law page, three-tier price table for litigation, cold calendar in a lawyer's hero, phone-only contact for a dentist, stock gavel or stock smile, Yelp badge, commercial logos, SOC 2, chat widget, countdown timers
- Proof form that wins: Google rating with count over the 10-review floor, then the credential line; case results for PI, before-and-after for cosmetic only
- Where price goes: on the page, as a flat fee, a "from" figure, contingency wording matching the ad, or a free consultation plus a stated process; quote-only allowed for litigation and complex tax with the free consult named
- Mobile bar: legal Call left and Free case review (form) right; dental Call left and Book online right; accountant Call left and Get a quote right
- Form field count: 4, plus one practice-area or service chip
- Benchmark median: LocaliQ 2026 legal 5.55% CVR and $131.63 CPL, dentists 10.67% and $72.97; LocaliQ healthcare general dentistry 7.74%, emergency 8.89%, orthodontics 14.21%; Unbounce legal 6.3%; Ruler legal 7.9% with 56.3% of conversions as calls

**Model 3 - agency or B2B service**
- Required layers: outcome headline with a number, three named case studies with number, timeframe and client, third-party review profile with score and count (Clutch, G2, Google), qualification form of four to six fields with chips, calendar immediately after a qualified submission, price statement ("from", minimum engagement or range), FAQ covering price, contract, who does the work and ownership, desktop layout given equal care, thank-you page
- Optional layers: client logo strip (only recognisable, real clients), platform partner badges below the fold, founder video, client-on-camera video case study, audit or calculator lead magnet, SOC 2 near the form for dev shops with enterprise buyers, phone number in the legal line
- Wrong layers: phone as primary CTA, licence and insurance in the hero, Yelp or BBB badges, before-and-after gallery on a consultancy, portfolio grid on a PPC or SEO agency, "as seen on" press logos in place of outcomes, cold calendar with no qualification, textarea, countdown timers, "free trial" language
- Proof form that wins: the case study with a number and a timeframe, beside a linked third-party review score with count
- Where price goes: on the page as "from $X/month", a minimum engagement or a three-tier typical range; quote-only only with the range stated
- Mobile bar: Book a call (form) left, See results (case-study anchor) right
- Form field count: 4 to 6, budget and company size as chips, multi-step allowed
- Benchmark median: LocaliQ 2026 business services 4.85% CVR and $93.69 CPL; Unbounce professional services 6.1%; Ruler professional services 6.1% with 95% forms in marketing; Chili Piper form-to-meeting 30% baseline and 66.7% with instant booking

**Model 4 - e-commerce or physical product**
- Required layers: product images including in-scale and on-model shots, price with shipping and tax estimated on the page, add-to-cart above the fold, sticky add-to-cart bar on mobile (drawer pattern), on-page reviews with count, distribution and customer photos, returns policy visible or linked, shipping time stated, third-party rating (Google, Trustpilot, Amazon), FAQ covering shipping, returns and sizing, express pay at checkout, guest checkout
- Optional layers: product-in-use video in the gallery (click to play), two-line founder note below the buy box, UGC video, product certifications beside the product, discount-for-email on desktop exit only, Q&A block, trust seal at the payment step
- Wrong layers: portfolio or case-study grid, calendar, consult form, founder video above the product, licence or insurance lines, commercial logo wall, "call now", stock lifestyle shots in place of product photos, trust seals in the hero, price or shipping hidden until checkout
- Proof form that wins: on-page reviews with photos and count, plus a third-party rating (brand-owned review pages trusted by 46%, Google 65%, Amazon 73%)
- Where price goes: stated, always, with total cost on the product page; "from" only for variant ranges
- Mobile bar: Add to cart with price and variant as the one button; a second button only for express pay
- Form field count: 0 before purchase; checkout with guest option and no account wall
- Benchmark median: Unbounce e-commerce 4.2% for the page action; Littledata Shopify 1.4% average and 3.2% top 20% for purchase; add-to-cart 4.6% median, 5.7% mobile, 9.8% desktop; Baymard 70.22% cart abandonment

**Model 5 - SaaS, app, course or community**
- Required layers: outcome headline, trial or demo CTA above the fold chosen by time to value, price or pricing model on the page (course and community: price and payment plan), interactive demo or 60-second product video above the fold or one scroll down, third-party rating with count (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot), three dated customer or student results with numbers, FAQ covering pricing, cancellation, security and onboarding (course: refund, time, access, who it is not for), thank-you page with the calendar for demo requests
- Optional layers: real client logo strip, SOC 2, ISO and GDPR badges near the form, founder video (course sales video), webinar registration for courses over about $500, card-required trial as a quality lever, phone number in the FAQ for enterprise or regulated buyers, community member count
- Wrong layers: licence or insurance line, "call now" hero, Yelp or BBB badges, before-and-after gallery, cold calendar in the hero, quote-only pricing for self-serve, long sales video for a cheap tool, portfolio grid (except template or design tools), stock office photos, countdown timers on evergreen offers, service-area map, same-day promise
- Proof form that wins: the product itself (ungated interactive demo or trial) plus a third-party rating with count; for courses, dated student results with numbers
- Where price goes: on the page for self-serve ("from $X per seat per month"); the pricing model named for sales-led; price and payment plan always for courses and communities
- Mobile bar: Start free trial (or Book a demo) left, See it work right; course Enrol left, Watch the preview right
- Form field count: trial 1 to 2 (email plus password, or SSO); demo 4 to 6 with company size as a chip; course checkout only
- Benchmark median: Unbounce SaaS 3.8%, online courses 18.3%, education 8.4%; LocaliQ 2026 education 13.14% CVR and $77.48 CPL; Ruler software 7.6%; FPS PPC visitor to lead 0.7%; ChartMogul median free-to-paid 8%; Livestorm show-up 48%

## Rules for the business-fit layer

- model is required input; if unset, infer from the conversion action on the page (tel: in hero → local; add-to-cart → ecommerce; trial or demo → saas; form plus calendar → agency) and print the inference for confirmation
- model=local-trade and hero lacks (licen[cs]e|insured|bonded) followed by a number → missing required layer
- model=local-trade and page has a logo strip whose logos are not manufacturer certifications → wrong layer (commercial logos on a residential page)
- model=local-trade and page has a case-study block with percentage outcomes and no photos → wrong layer (B2B format)
- model=local-trade and page has a calendar embed → wrong layer; calendar belongs on /thank-you
- model=local-trade and price token absent ($\d, "from $", "call-out", "free quote") → missing required layer (78% more likely to call with pricing online)
- model=local-trade and subtype in (renovation, landscaping, painting, roofing, flooring) and no before-and-after or project gallery → optional layer missing, flag not fail
- model=local-trade and subtype in (emergency plumbing, drain, electrical fault, lockout) and page has a gallery above the form → wrong order; speed and licence first
- model=local-trade and first-viewport has no tel: link → missing required layer
- model=local-trade and mobile bar has "Book" instead of "Call" → wrong bar; Call left, form right
- model=local-professional and subtype=legal and first-viewport has no tel: link → missing required layer (56.3% of legal conversions are calls)
- model=local-professional and subtype in (dental, clinic, physio) and no online booking path (widget or form-then-calendar) → missing required layer (43% prefer to book online)
- model=local-professional and subtype=legal and calendar embed in first viewport → wrong layer; qualification before the slot
- model=local-professional and no credential line (bar|board certified|DDS|CPA|years) within the first two screens → missing required layer
- model=local-professional and page shows a review count under 10 → flag (52% of dental buyers need 10 reviews)
- model=local-professional and price statement absent and no "free consultation" or "no fee unless" line → missing required layer
- model=local-professional and subtype=legal and superlative claim present ("best", "#1", "top-rated") → check against bar rules, not CRO
- model=local-professional and page has SOC 2, ISO or "trusted by N companies" → wrong layer
- model=local-professional and subtype in (dental, clinic) and no insurance-accepted line or logos → optional layer missing, flag
- model=local-professional and before-and-after gallery present and subtype not in (cosmetic dentistry, aesthetics, dermatology, orthodontics) → wrong layer
- model=agency and tel: link is the primary hero CTA → wrong layer (95.1% of agency conversions are forms)
- model=agency and no case study with a number and a timeframe → missing required layer
- model=agency and no third-party review link (clutch.com, g2.com, google.com/maps) with score and count → missing required layer
- model=agency and calendar embed in first viewport without a preceding form → wrong order; form, then calendar for qualified submissions
- model=agency and form has fewer than 4 fields and no qualification chip → flag (qualification earns its place here); more than 6 fields or a textarea → fail
- model=agency and price token absent ("from $", "minimum", "typical range") → missing required layer
- model=agency and licence or insurance line in the hero → wrong layer
- model=agency and before-and-after or portfolio grid present and subtype not in (design, branding, video, web design) → wrong layer
- model=agency and desktop layout not audited → incomplete audit (desktop 11.6% against mobile 8.3%)
- model=agency and chat widget script present → flag, load after interaction and desktop only if kept
- model=ecommerce and page has a portfolio grid, case-study block, calendar embed or consult form → wrong layer
- model=ecommerce and add-to-cart button not in first viewport at 390x844 → missing required layer
- model=ecommerce and no fixed-position add-to-cart element on scroll at mobile width → optional layer missing, flag (tested lifts +5.2% to +7.9%)
- model=ecommerce and price present but no shipping estimate or total-cost line on the page → missing required layer (67% of sites fail; extra costs are 40% of abandonment)
- model=ecommerce and no returns-policy text or link on the page → missing required layer (44% fail; 15% abandon over returns)
- model=ecommerce and rating average shown without a count or a distribution → fail
- model=ecommerce and reviews block has no customer photos → flag (users validate site photos against customer images)
- model=ecommerce and licence, insurance, bonded, or "same-day" service line present → wrong layer
- model=ecommerce and trust seal in the hero rather than at payment → wrong placement
- model=ecommerce and a founder video sits above the product images → wrong order
- model=ecommerce and tel: link in the hero → flag; not the action for this model
- model=saas and licence, insurance or bonded line present → wrong layer
- model=saas and tel: link is the primary hero CTA → wrong layer (self-serve); allowed in FAQ for enterprise
- model=saas and no trial or demo CTA in the first viewport → missing required layer
- model=saas and no product demo (interactive embed or video element) within the first two screens → missing required layer (80% of top demo CTAs are above the fold)
- model=saas and demo embed is gated with more than one field → flag (66% of top demos are ungated)
- model=saas and no price token and no pricing-model phrase (per seat, per user, per month, usage-based) → missing required layer
- model=saas and calendar embed in first viewport without a form → wrong order
- model=saas and Yelp, BBB or Angi badge present → wrong layer
- model=saas and before-and-after gallery present → wrong layer
- model=saas and security badges (SOC 2, ISO, GDPR, HIPAA) present but not within one screen of the form → wrong placement
- model=course and price absent → fail; payment plan absent → flag
- model=course and no dated student result with a number → missing required layer
- model=course and countdown timer present on an evergreen page → fail (misrepresentation policy)

- model=community and no member count or activity proof → missing required layer

- any model and rating average shown without a count → fail (prior rule, restated because it holds in all five)

- any model and a "did not survive" number appears in the audit output → strip it
