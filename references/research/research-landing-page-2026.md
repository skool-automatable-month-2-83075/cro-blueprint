## Findings

### 1. Form design

- Under 5 options, use radios or chips, not a drop-down; over 10, use a text or autocomplete field. Baymard, 200,000+ testing hours, updated Jan 2025 (a). Radios finished 2.5 s faster in a 708-person controlled test, p under 0.05, desktop only (b, Speero 2016). This confirms the cro-gaps ruling: any qualifying question on the page is chips or radios, never a select or textarea
- Autofill: users who autofill abandon 75% less and finish 35% faster, Chrome telemetry over millions of page loads, correlational and self-selected (a for sample, b for causality; Chrome 2024). It only fires when the field has a real name or id, sits inside a form element with a submit control, and carries a correct token. first-name is wrong and fails silently; given-name, family-name, email, tel, postal-code are the tokens (a, MDN, web.dev)
- type="tel" plus a single unsplit field is required for both autofill and the numeric keypad (a, web.dev Learn Forms; Baymard split-field rule carried over from the August dossier)
- Mark BOTH required and optional. Only 14% of sites do; marking only optional fields produced a validation error for 32% of test users (b, Baymard 2018, reconfirmed through 2021). On a four-field all-required form the cheapest compliant form is a single line "All fields required" above the form
- Validate on blur, not on keystroke and not only on submit. Fixed-length fields (phone, postcode) may validate the moment the length is reached. Show a positive tick, clear the error when fixed, never validate a field the user has not left. 31% of sites have no inline validation at all (b, Baymard Jan 2024)
- Phone field abandonment is 6.28% mean, 3.29% median across 1,028 forms, third behind password and email (a, Zuko 1,362 forms). The "37% abandon on a required phone field" line in the August dossier does not appear in Zuko's own data and is retired below
- Overall form starter-to-completion is 51.7% across about 93 million sessions; mobile 47.5% vs desktop 55.5% (a, Zuko 2025). A mobile form losing more than about half its starters is average, not broken; below that, look at the form
- Multi-step vs single: Zuko's own guidance is single step at 2 to 5 fields, multi-step only at 6+, and it cites no data of its own; the 86% is still HubSpot's marketer survey (c). With the four-field house spec, single step is correct and multi-step is not a test worth running
- Phone-reason line: the Baymard 1,026-shopper finding (14% never give a number, 39% of sites ask without saying why) from the cro-gaps file stands; nothing new either way. Keep the one-line reason under the phone field (b)
- Consent line, TCPA status as of September 2026: the one-to-one rule is dead (11th Cir. Jan 2025, FCC final rule Sep 2025). Prior express written consent still means written, signed, naming the business and the number, disclosing autodialed or prerecorded marketing calls and texts, and stating consent is not a condition of purchase (a, FCC via CFS Law Monitor). The Fifth Circuit held in Feb 2026 that the written-consent rule exceeds the statute, but that binds only Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi courts and creates a split, so the form keeps the full disclosure everywhere (b, TCPA Blog). Unchecked box, above the button, consent not required to submit, consent text and timestamp logged

### 2. Proof

- Count beats average: 4.5 stars from 57 ratings was chosen nearly 2 to 1 over 5.0 from 4, across 5,170+ survey respondents (b, Baymard 2023, refreshed 2025). Never render a star average without its count and source
- Self-hosted testimonials are read as biased; external review platforms are trusted more; upfront disclosure of price, fees and contact details is one of four credibility factors (b, NN/g 2016, qualitative). So the rating line links to the live Google profile, and the written testimonials name the platform they came from
- Video testimonial count: no study found that varies the number of videos. Wyzowl's 85% "convinced to buy by a video" and the 77% testimonial figure are self-report surveys, not tests (c). The Unbounce/Vidyard lightbox result (6.5% to 13%) is one page, one test (b, single). The only evidence on quantity is the review literature in the cro-gaps file: gains flatten after five to ten. Three well-attributed videos remains the rule; nine stays a test
- Founder video: no controlled test found anywhere in this pass. Foundry CRO's own 2026 roundup calls the founder-video effect a "pattern observation" with no benchmark (c). Keep it, but its cost is real: the poster is the LCP element and the video must be click-to-play. One single test put click-to-play at 41.2% vs autoplay 9.7% (c, Vmaker via Foundry, no sample)
- Logo walls: nothing new; the cro-gaps verdict (no credible controlled evidence, TRUSTe removal +12.6%) stands. Licence, insurance and bond line with the real number replaces the six-logo wall for residential trades (b)
- Badges: the only evidence that applies is the count-plus-source finding above. A badge is a rating line with a platform mark; a badge with no number is decoration

### 3. Above the fold

- Message match is "how well your landing page copy matches the phrasing of the ad" in Unbounce's own glossary, meaning and phrasing rather than an identical string (b, Unbounce). This supports the cro-gaps softening: continuity of meaning, the searcher's service and city present, no keyword-stuffed H1. The 66% and 212% numbers stay in the August dossier as single tests
- Sticky bottom CTA bar now has data. Across 33 A/B tests, sticky elements won 29% of the time on mobile vs 15% on desktop, won 100% of the time on cart and checkout pages, and 0% on homepages, list pages and other orientation pages (b, Online Dialogue 2023). A CRE client saw +25% sales from a sticky bar whose label lowered commitment (c, no sample). The "Contentsquare 58 million sessions, +31%" number circulating in 2026 could not be found on any Contentsquare page or on the vendor page that search attributed it to (hollow). Ruling: mobile bar stays, mobile only, and its label should read as low-commitment ("Call now", "Get my quote"), because the lift came from the label as much as the stickiness
- Two buttons in the bar (call and text): no new test either way; the Leadferno age-split argument from the cro-gaps file remains the reason
- Chatbots: no controlled evidence that a chat widget lifts lead-gen conversion on a paid landing page. The 3x and 23% vendor numbers trace to unpublished "research" with no method (c, Whitehat/Dashly). The one academic paper found is B2B and unreadable behind a paywall (snippet only). What is measurable is cost: chat widgets ship 100 to 500 KB of JavaScript on the main thread (b, PageSpeedFix 2026 measurements). Ruling: no chat widget on an ad page; if the owner insists, load it after first interaction and only on desktop
- No popups and no header nav: nothing new beyond the August dossier; Contentsquare's 40% of visits containing a frustration signal and scroll rates near 50% argue for the offer, proof and CTA all inside the first viewport (a, 90 billion sessions)
- Price on page: NN/g watched B2B users leave for competitors when price was missing and rank price the number one information need (b, 2013); when exact price is impossible, show prices for representative jobs (b, 2006). Old, qualitative, consistent with Baymard's 40% cost-abandonment figure in the cro-gaps file. The blueprint's "What it costs" section is now graded (b), not house rule
- Click-to-call numbers: every figure in CallRail's 2024 landing-page guide is second-hand (200%, 10 to 15x, 25 to 40%) and none traces to a stated sample (c). The rule that a tel: link exists above the fold rests on Google's own call-conversion mechanics and the August dossier's 62% click-to-call share, not on these

### 4. Speed

- The founder video's poster is the LCP element when it sits in the hero. Since Chrome 116 the first painted frame counts even without a poster, so a video with no poster makes LCP wait for the video stream (a, web.dev; b, DebugBear). Rules: real poster, exact aspect ratio, preloaded with fetchpriority="high", never lazy-loaded, preload="none" on the video itself, no autoplay (autoplay downloads immediately even off-screen)
- LCP budget split: TTFB about 40%, resource load delay under 10%, load duration about 40%, render delay under 10% (a, web.dev 2025). A 2.5 s target on a 600 ms TTFB leaves about 1 s for the poster to download, which caps it at roughly 100 to 150 KB on 4G
- YouTube or Vimeo iframes for testimonials block the main thread over 1.7 s on the median site and 4.5 s on the worst 10% of mobile sites (a, Chrome DevRel via web.dev and PageSpeedFix). Self-host MP4 with posters, or use a facade that loads the player on click
- INP 200 ms at p75; interaction is input delay plus processing plus presentation; long tasks over 50 ms are the enemy and third-party scripts are the usual cause (a, web.dev 2025). A page carrying a testing script, a replay script, a chat widget and nine video players is exactly the profile that fails
- Session replay cost is small in bytes (Clarity about 17 KB gzipped, Hotjar about 50 KB) but non-zero in CPU on budget phones (c, vendor comparisons). Load replay and testing scripts async and after the LCP; never sync in the head
- Speed to lead-gen conversion, corrected wording: a 0.1 s gain was associated with 21.6% more visitors progressing to the form submission page and 5.5% more moving from homepage to form step one, on lead-gen sites, across 37 brands and 30 million sessions, observational (a for sample, b for causality; Deloitte/Google 2020). The August dossier's "8.3% better lead-gen bounce" was not on the web.dev case-study page opened this pass; quote the 21.6% and say "associated with"
- Field vs lab: PSI field data is CrUX at p75 over 28 days and falls back to origin when the URL is thin; lab is a Moto G4 emulation (a, Google). A brand-new ad page has no CrUX, so the audit uses lab LCP with an explicit "lab, not field" label until the origin has 28 days of traffic

### 6. Auditing an existing page automatically

- What a headless browser can detect from the 18 components, and how: tel: links in the first viewport (querySelectorAll a[href^="tel:"], filter by getBoundingClientRect().top under viewport height at 390x844); form presence, field count, input types, autocomplete tokens, required attributes, label association (label[for] or wrapping label), presence of a select or textarea; a fixed-position element at the bottom of the mobile viewport containing a tel: link (computed position fixed or sticky, bottom within 0 to 8 px); header and footer elements and the count of internal anchors outside the form (a[href] not tel:, not mailto:, not # or the form id); meta robots noindex on the landing page and the thank-you page; robots.txt not disallowing the path; video elements with poster attributes, autoplay attributes, preload values, and iframes from youtube.com or vimeo.com; rating text via regex on innerText (\d\.\d\s*(/5|stars|out of 5) and (\d{2,})\s*(reviews|ratings)); a review-count number next to a star average; licence or insurance keywords (licen[cs]e|insured|bonded followed by a number); a price token ($\d or "from $" or "starting at"); a countdown timer (elements whose text matches \d{2}:\d{2} and changes between two snapshots); popups and interstitials (new fixed or dialog elements with area over 40% of the viewport appearing within 10 s or on scroll); third-party script inventory (performance.getEntriesByType("resource") grouped by host, flag chat and video-embed hosts); LCP, CLS and TBT in lab via Lighthouse navigation mode; INP only via timespan mode after scripted interactions, or via CrUX when the origin has data; total JS bytes and count of scripts in the head
- What needs a human or a language model: message match against the actual ad, offer strength, whether the proof is real and traceable to proof.md, reading level (measurable) versus whether the copy sells (not), whether the three selling points are differentiating, whether the FAQ answers the true objections, photo authenticity, whether the guarantee wording equals the ad's
- Speed via API: GET https://www.googleapis.com/pagespeedonline/v5/runPagespeed?url=URL&strategy=mobile&category=performance&key=KEY; read lighthouseResult.audits["largest-contentful-paint"].numericValue, ["cumulative-layout-shift"], ["total-blocking-time"], ["lcp-lazy-loaded"], ["third-party-summary"], ["prioritize-lcp-image"]; read loadingExperience.metrics.LARGEST_CONTENTFUL_PAINT_MS.percentile when present, else originLoadingExperience, else mark "no field data" (a, Google). Field data in the PSI API is being retired toward the CrUX API, so the script should call CrUX directly for p75 and PSI for lab
- Lighthouse user flows: navigation mode for the cold-load numbers; timespan mode wrapped around a scroll to the form and a tap on the first field to catch layout shift from lazy sections and the sticky bar; snapshot mode for accessibility and label checks after the page settles (a, web.dev)
- Rubrics worth borrowing from: Instapage's 100-point list is seven categories and mostly subjective, but its objective items map cleanly to the list above (form 3 to 5 fields, optional marking, input types, no conflicting CTAs, no broken links, load under 3 s, pop-up minimisation, trust signal near the form). SEOmator's weighted 172-rule 0 to 100 score is the right output shape: per-category weights published, every fail explained with the fix. Neither covers ad pages specifically; none of the "free landing page audit tools" found renders JavaScript and checks a tel: link in the viewport

### 7. Thank-you page

- The conversion action for a lead is set to count "One" per click; Google says so explicitly and gives leads as the example (a). A refresh, a back-button revisit or a second submission then cannot double count within the click
- transaction_id dedupes two hits with the same id and exists precisely for confirmation-page revisits (a). For a lead form, pass the CRM lead id or a UUID minted on submit as transaction_id; it is belt and braces on top of "One"
- noindex on /thank-you only works if the crawler can reach the page: never disallow it in robots.txt, and keep it out of the sitemap (a, Google Dec 2025)
- Calendar on the thank-you page: still no controlled evidence found this pass; the house ruling stands as (c). What the evidence does support is that the page fires exactly one conversion and that a booking is not a second one (a)
- Callback-time promise on the page: no new evidence that stating a time lifts anything; it stays only if the auto-dial actually meets it

## Machine-checkable rules for a live URL

- Viewport 390x844, mobile UA, network throttled to Lighthouse mobile defaults, page settled 5 s before DOM checks
- LP-01 tel link above fold: at least one a[href^="tel:"] with rect.top under 844 px and rect.height 24 px or more; pass 1 or more
- LP-02 tel href matches displayed number: digits of href equal digits of the element's innerText or of the nearest visible number; pass all
- LP-03 one phone number: distinct digit strings across all tel: hrefs; pass exactly 1
- LP-04 form present: at least one form element with a submit control; pass 1 or more
- LP-05 field count: visible input, select, textarea inside the primary form excluding hidden, checkbox and submit; pass 3 to 4, warn 5, fail 6 or more
- LP-06 field types: exactly one input[type="tel"], one input[type="email"] if email is collected, no select, no textarea; pass on all four
- LP-07 autocomplete tokens: every text field has autocomplete in {given-name, family-name, name, email, tel, postal-code, street-address}; pass 100%
- LP-08 labels: every field has label[for=id] or a wrapping label with non-empty text; placeholder-only fails; pass 100%
- LP-09 required marking: each field has the required attribute and either an asterisk in its label or an "All fields required" line within the form; pass
- LP-10 consent checkbox: an unchecked input[type="checkbox"] inside the form, not required, whose adjacent text contains the business name, "text" or "call", "not a condition", and "STOP"; pass
- LP-11 privacy link from form: an a[href] whose text or href contains "privacy" within the form's closest section; pass 1 or more
- LP-12 mobile sticky bar: an element with computed position fixed or sticky, bottom edge within 8 px of the viewport bottom, height 44 to 96 px, containing a tel: link; pass exactly 1
- LP-13 no header or footer nav: zero header, nav and footer elements containing a[href] to any path other than privacy and terms; pass 0
- LP-14 exit links: count of a[href] not tel:, not mailto:, not fragment, not privacy or terms; pass 0, warn 1 to 2
- LP-15 no popup: no element with position fixed or dialog[open] whose area exceeds 40% of the viewport appearing within 15 s of load or after a 3-screen scroll; pass 0
- LP-16 no chat widget: no script or iframe from intercom, drift, zendesk, crisp, tawk, tidio, hubspot chat, livechat hosts; pass 0
- LP-17 no third-party video iframe: no iframe src matching youtube, youtu.be, vimeo, wistia, loom; pass 0
- LP-18 video hygiene: every video element has poster, has no autoplay, and preload is none or metadata; pass 100%
- LP-19 LCP poster not lazy: the Lighthouse audit lcp-lazy-loaded passes and the LCP element is an image or a video poster with fetchpriority high or a matching link[rel=preload]; pass
- LP-20 lab LCP: Lighthouse mobile largest-contentful-paint numericValue 2500 ms or less; pass, warn to 4000, fail over
- LP-21 lab CLS: cumulative-layout-shift 0.1 or less in navigation mode AND in a timespan covering a scroll to the form; pass
- LP-22 lab TBT as INP proxy: total-blocking-time 200 ms or less; pass, warn to 600; where CrUX exists, INP p75 200 ms or less
- LP-23 field data label: if loadingExperience and originLoadingExperience are both absent, the report says "lab only, no field data"; never print a field pass without CrUX
- LP-24 rating with count: innerText matches a star average (\d\.\d) within 80 characters of a count (\d{2,}\s*(reviews|ratings)) and a platform word (Google, Yelp, Facebook, Trustpilot, Houzz, Angi); pass 1 or more, and the average is not "5.0" when the count is under 20 without a link to the profile
- LP-25 rating link: the rating block contains an a[href] to google.com/maps, g.page, yelp.com, facebook.com or trustpilot.com; pass
- LP-26 review count: number of distinct testimonial blocks (elements containing a quote or blockquote with a name); pass 5 or more
- LP-27 licence line: innerText matches (licen[cs]e|insured|bonded) followed within 40 characters by a number or "#"; pass
- LP-28 price present: innerText matches \$\s?\d or (from|starting at)\s+\$ or "free quote" or "call-out fee"; pass 1 or more
- LP-29 no countdown: no element whose text matches \d{1,2}:\d{2}(:\d{2})? and changes between two snapshots 3 s apart; pass 0
- LP-30 word count: visible body text word count; HVAC and emergency trades pass 250 or under, home services 400 or under, complex services 600 or under
- LP-31 reading level: Flesch-Kincaid grade on visible copy 7 or under; pass
- LP-32 noindex on the landing page: meta[name=robots] content includes noindex, or X-Robots-Tag header; pass
- LP-33 robots.txt does not disallow the landing page path or /thank-you; pass
- LP-34 thank-you page: /thank-you or the form's redirect target returns 200, carries noindex, has no form, no nav, contains a tel: link and text matching "minute"; pass
- LP-35 conversion fires once: on a scripted submit, exactly one request to googleads.g.doubleclick.net/pagead/conversion or google.com/pagead/1p-conversion with the lead label, and zero on a reload of the thank-you page (the tag may still fire on reload if the site relies on "One" counting, so record it as warn not fail); pass
- LP-36 gclid persisted: after loading URL?gclid=test, sessionStorage or the form's hidden inputs contain gclid; pass
- LP-37 phone snippet registered: after loading with ?gclid=test, window.google_wcc_status is defined; pass
- LP-38 third-party weight: total bytes from hosts other than the page's origin and Google tag hosts under 300 KB transferred before LCP; pass, warn to 600 KB
- LP-39 head scripts: no synchronous script in head other than the Google tag; pass
- LP-40 touch targets: every a and button in the first two viewports has rect width and height 24 px or more, the primary CTAs 44 px or more; pass
- LP-41 contrast: body text contrast 4.5 to 1 via the Lighthouse color-contrast audit; pass
- LP-42 alt text: every img inside proof sections has non-empty alt; pass
- Human-only flags the script prints for review: message match against the ad group headline, offer specificity, guarantee wording equals the ad, photo authenticity, proof traceability to proof.md, FAQ covers price, timing, guarantee, service-area truth

## Rules for /audit

- Qualifying questions, when any exist, are chips or radios; never a select, never a textarea (a)
- Every field: type set (tel, email, text), single unsplit phone input, correct autocomplete token, real label above, name and id set, inside a form with a submit button (a)
- Say "All fields required" once above the form, or mark each; never leave marking implicit (b)
- Validate on blur with a positive tick; phone and postcode may validate at full length; never while typing; keep the entry on error (b)
- One-line reason under the phone field: "So we can call you back about your quote" (b)
- Consent: unchecked checkbox above the button, business named, calls and texts named, "not a condition of purchase", STOP and HELP, privacy link; consent text, timestamp and IP logged; keep the full wording in every state despite the Fifth Circuit (a)
- Rating line = platform + average + count + link to the live profile; never an average without its count; three attributed video testimonials, more only if they exist and load click-to-play (b)
- Licence, insurance and bond line with the real number in the hero, in place of a logo wall for residential trades (b)
- A "What it costs" block with a real figure: call-out fee, typical range, or priced example jobs (b)
- H1 carries the ad's service and the searcher's city with continuity of meaning; not a string copy of the keyword (b)
- Mobile bottom bar: one, fixed, call left and quote right, labels low-commitment; nothing sticky on desktop (b)
- No chat widget, no third-party video iframe, no popup, no header or footer nav, no countdown (a)/(b)
- Founder and testimonial videos: self-hosted MP4, real poster at exact aspect ratio, poster preloaded with fetchpriority high, preload="none", no autoplay, never lazy-loaded when in the hero (a)
- Lab targets before launch: LCP 2.5 s or under (aim 1.8), CLS 0.1 or under including after scroll, TBT 200 ms or under; replay and testing scripts async after LCP; under 300 KB third-party before LCP (a)
- Report speed as "lab" until the origin has 28 days of CrUX, then hold p75 LCP, INP and CLS to the good thresholds (a)
- Thank-you page: own URL, noindex, crawlable, out of the sitemap, no form, no nav, tel link, callback time; lead conversion action counts "One" and passes a transaction_id (a)
- Benchmark the page against its LocaliQ 2025 vertical median, not the all-industry 8.18%: plumbing 7.63%, AC 6.56%, heating 7.48%, roofing 3.70%, electricians 9.08%, cleaning 17.65%, handyman 13.45% (a)
- Run the 42 machine checks above on every live URL before calling it done; print the human-only flags for review rather than guessing at them (house rule)
